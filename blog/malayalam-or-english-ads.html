Malayalam or English: which language should your ad use?

The right language depends on who you want to reach and what you are selling. Here is a practical way to decide, and how to make either choice work.

By Ad Pro Advertising · 18 November 2026 · 4 min read

The short version

  • Start with the customer, not your own preference.
  • Keep one message on each poster or reel.
  • Make the offer clear with the sound off.
  • Test both versions and judge them on enquiries.

Start with who is reading

Picture the person who decides to buy. A parent choosing a course for their child, a family arranging care for an elderly relative and an IT manager ordering equipment may all read differently.

Ask your front-office staff what language customers use when they call or message. Look through your WhatsApp enquiries too. Your own customers are better evidence than any general rule.

When Malayalam usually makes sense

  • Your customers are mostly local households.
  • The message is emotional or depends on trust, such as healthcare, family services or education for children.
  • You are explaining an offer that must be understood instantly.
  • You want a voice-over that sounds like someone from the area.

When English may work better

  • You sell to other businesses or to professionals.
  • Your audience reaches beyond Kerala.
  • Customers normally search for the product by its English name, as with many software courses and technical services.

Mixing both, without the clutter

Many good Kerala ads use both languages, with each doing one job. A Malayalam headline can carry the message, while English handles the terms people already use, such as "Tally", "Python" or "WhatsApp". Keep your phone number and brand name easy to spot.

Avoid saying everything twice on one poster. That halves the space and makes both versions harder to read. If you need full versions in each language, make two creatives.

Get the Malayalam right

  • Write it, do not machine-translate it. Literal translations can sound stiff or simply wrong.
  • Use a font made for Malayalam, and check that every conjunct letter displays correctly.
  • Allow more room. Malayalam text can need more height and space than the English layout allowed for.
  • Have a native reader check the final artwork, not just the typed copy.

Plan reels for silent viewing

Many people scroll with the sound off, so your on-screen text must tell the story alone. A voice-over then adds warmth for those listening. In a short reel, keep it simple:

  1. Show what the customer gets in the first moments.
  2. Give one reason to believe it.
  3. End with one action and your phone number on screen.

Use licensed or royalty-free music only. Film songs can get your ad blocked and create copyright trouble.

Test it instead of guessing

Run a Malayalam and an English version with the same offer, audience and budget. Give each its own WhatsApp opening message, so you can see which one brings real enquiries. Judge the result on genuine interest, not likes.

Examples by type of business

  • Training centre: a Malayalam headline can speak to parents and students, while course names such as "Tally" stay in English.
  • Home-care service: Malayalam can feel reassuring for families in Kerala. If you also serve relatives abroad or customers in another city, make a separate version for them.
  • Manufacturer or wholesaler: English product names and sizes may be clearer for buyers, with Malayalam used for a local offer.
  • Shop or restaurant: short, warm Malayalam can work well for neighbourhood offers and festival greetings.

Treat these as starting points. Your own customers and results should have the final say.

A quick check before you publish

  1. Can someone understand the offer in a few seconds?
  2. Does the design carry one main message?
  3. Is the text large enough on a phone?
  4. Has a fluent reader checked the spelling?
  5. Are the phone number, place and dates correct?
  6. Do the poster, reel and website all say the same thing?
  7. Do you have the right to use the music and images?

Common questions

Should we use Malayalam written in English letters?

It can feel casual and familiar in chats and some social posts. It is usually harder to read in longer lines, so use it sparingly and test it. For a formal message, proper Malayalam script or clear English is often the safer choice.

Do we need a separate reel for each language?

If each language needs its own voice-over and full text, yes. For a simple offer, one reel can use a Malayalam headline with familiar English product names.

Which sizes should we make?

Plan for square feed posts and vertical stories or reels at minimum. Keep important text away from the edges, where app buttons can cover it.
