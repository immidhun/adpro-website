How to rebuild your website without losing Google rankings

A new website can quietly wipe out years of search visibility if old pages simply disappear. The fix is careful planning before anything goes live.

By Ad Pro Advertising · 18 November 2026 · 4 min read

The short version

  • List every existing page before you change anything.
  • Keep the same address wherever you can.
  • Send each old address to its closest new page with a 301 redirect.
  • Keep redirects for at least a year, and expect some short-term movement.

Why rebuilds lose traffic

Google knows your site as a set of addresses. If /courses/tally.html ranks today and vanishes tomorrow, searchers land on an error page and Google has no clear replacement to show. The new design may look better, but the page that earned the ranking is gone.

Step 1: List everything you have now

Before any design work, collect every address on the current site. Use your sitemap, Google Search Console, analytics and a crawl of the site. Include images and PDFs that people may link to.

Mark the pages that matter most: those that bring in visitors, have links from other websites or lead to enquiries.

Step 2: Keep addresses where you can

The safest redirect is the one you never need. If a page keeps its purpose, try to keep its exact address on the new site, even if the design changes completely.

When we rebuilt the G-TEC Computer Education, Charummood website, all 28 existing pages were carried across. 21 kept their exact address, and the rest were permanently redirected.

Step 3: Map the old to the new

Make a simple spreadsheet with two columns: old address and new address. Every old page gets a row. Then check three things:

  • Each old page points to the closest matching new page.
  • Redirects go straight to the final page, with no chains.
  • You are not sending many old pages to the home page. Google specifically advises against redirecting lots of old addresses to an unrelated page.

Step 4: Use permanent redirects

Set up server-side 301 redirects. They tell browsers and Google that the page has moved for good. Google recommends keeping them in place for at least one year, so do not delete them after launch.

Step 5: Carry the content across

A redirect cannot save a page whose useful content was removed. Keep the details that made it rank: service information, answers to common questions, fees where you publish them, and location details. Copy across page titles and descriptions too, then improve them with care.

Step 6: Check launch day

  1. Test the old addresses from your spreadsheet. Each should land on the right new page.
  2. Make sure the new site is not blocked from search engines. A leftover "noindex" tag from the test site is a common mistake.
  3. Submit the new sitemap in Search Console.
  4. Confirm your phone links, WhatsApp buttons and forms all work.
  5. Update the website link on your Google Business Profile if the address changed.

If you are also moving to a new domain, use the Change of Address tool in Search Console.

What to expect afterwards

Even a well-run move can cause some ranking changes while Google recrawls the site. Google says this can take a few weeks or more for a medium-sized site. So avoid launching just before your busiest season, and watch Search Console for pages reporting errors.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Deleting old pages because they look outdated. Update them or redirect them to a true replacement.
  • Changing the design, addresses, content and domain all at once without a plan. The more that changes, the harder any drop is to trace.
  • Leaving the test site open to Google. Protect it so unfinished pages do not appear in search.
  • Forgetting images and PDFs. Brochures and price lists may have their own links and visitors.
  • Removing redirects when you change hosting. Add them to your launch checklist for every future move.

Common questions

What if an old page has no equivalent on the new site?

First ask whether it should have one. If people still need that information, keep or rewrite it. If the service has truly ended and nothing similar exists, let the address return a proper "not found" response. Do not send it to the home page just to avoid an error.

Is changing only my hosting risky?

It is usually simpler, because the addresses and content stay the same. Still copy everything carefully, check that redirects and security settings came across, and test the site before and after pointing the domain to the new server.

Can I change my domain name at the same time?

You can, but plan it with extra care. Redirect each old page to its match on the new domain, verify both domains in Search Console and use the Change of Address tool. Update your Google profile, social pages and printed material as well.

What should I check in the weeks after launch?

Watch the page indexing and performance reports in Search Console. Look for important pages that have dropped out, unexpected "not found" errors and redirects that fail. Above all, compare your calls and WhatsApp enquiries with the period before launch.
