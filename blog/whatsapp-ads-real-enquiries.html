WhatsApp ads: how to tell which enquiries are real

A cheap conversation looks great in Ads Manager. But a chat is not a customer. Here is a simple way to see which WhatsApp ads actually bring in business.

By Ad Pro Advertising · 18 November 2026 · 4 min read

The short version

  • A "conversation started" only tells you someone opened a chat.
  • A simple enquiry sheet shows what happened next.
  • Give each ad its own opening message so you can tell them apart.
  • On small budgets, wait for a pattern before changing things.

What the ad platform can and cannot see

Click-to-WhatsApp ads open a chat with your business when someone taps the ad on Facebook or Instagram. That makes them a natural fit for local businesses, because customers can ask a question in the app they already use.

Meta can report how many people clicked and started a conversation. Unless your WhatsApp system sends results back to Meta, it does not know what happened inside that chat. It cannot tell whether the person was serious, visited your shop or paid you. Your team knows that.

Keep a simple enquiry sheet

You do not need expensive software to start. A shared Google Sheet is enough. Add one row for each enquiry:

  • Date
  • Name and phone number
  • Which ad or offer they came from
  • What they asked for
  • Status: replied, interested, visited, bought or not interested
  • Reason, if the enquiry was lost

Keep the sheet private, because it holds customer details. Limit access to the staff who need it.

Make every ad identifiable

You can set the first message that appears in the customer's chat. Use a different one for each campaign. For example:

  • "Hi, I'd like details of the Onam offer."
  • "Hi, I'd like to know about the weekend batch."

When the message arrives, staff can tell which ad brought that person in and record it. It is a small step, and it makes the sheet far more reliable.

Reply fast, and the same way every time

A paid enquiry can go cold while it waits. Decide who answers, during which hours and what happens after closing time. Use saved replies for common questions, but let a person take over as soon as the conversation needs one.

Make sure every reply matches your website and posters. If an ad offers one thing and staff quote another, you lose trust and the sale.

Compare ads on what they actually produce

At the end of each week, line up your ad spend against the sheet. Work out three figures for each campaign:

  1. Cost per conversation: what the platform shows you.
  2. Cost per interested enquiry: spend divided by people who were genuinely interested.
  3. Cost per customer: spend divided by people who bought.

The ad with the cheapest conversations is not always the winner. One that brings fewer chats but more buyers is usually the better ad.

Do not overreact to one week

With only a few enquiries a week, one good or bad week means very little. Wait until you see a consistent pattern before you cut an audience or move your budget. And turn off offer ads the day the offer ends, so people are not messaging about a deal that has gone.

Ask better first questions

Your first reply can do two jobs: help the customer and show how serious the enquiry is. Keep it friendly, and ask only what you need to give a useful answer:

  • Which product, course or service are you looking for?
  • Where are you located?
  • When do you need it?
  • Is this for you or for someone else?

Avoid sending a long form as your first message. Answer their question first, then ask one thing at a time.

Should the ad go to WhatsApp or your website?

Use WhatsApp when people usually want to ask a question before deciding, and you have staff ready to reply. Use a website page when the buyer needs detail, such as a full course outline or product range, or when you want them to read before they contact you.

Many local businesses use both. The important thing is to record where each enquiry came from, so you can compare them fairly.

Follow up without becoming a nuisance

Agree a simple rule with your team: for example, one helpful follow-up after an unanswered quote, then leave it. Note the outcome in your sheet. Only send offers to people who have agreed to hear from you, and stop when someone asks you to.

Common questions

Why am I getting many chats but few customers?

Look at the whole path. The ad may be too broad or the offer unclear. The audience may be outside your area. Replies may be slow, or the price and details may only become clear late in the chat. Your enquiry sheet will show where people drop away.

Do I need the WhatsApp Business API?

Not to start. The WhatsApp Business app can be enough for a small team. Consider the API when you need automation, several staff handling chats or results sent back to your ad account.
