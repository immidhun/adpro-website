How to show live attendance on your training centre's TV

The TV in your lobby can do more than play the news. It can show who has arrived for each batch, then promote your courses while the room is quiet.

By Ad Pro Advertising · 18 November 2026 · 3 min read

The short version

  • Tutors mark attendance on a phone and the TV updates.
  • Show only what is needed. A lobby is a public place.
  • Separate "on leave" from "not arrived yet".
  • Between batches, use the screen for your own ads and notices.

Why put attendance on a screen?

At the start of each batch, front-office staff often face the same questions. Has this student come? Is the tutor marking attendance? Who is on leave? A live board in the lobby makes the answer visible to everyone, without anyone leaving the desk.

It also shows parents and visitors that the centre is organised. That matters when someone is sitting in your lobby deciding whether to join.

What a good board shows

  • The batch name and start time
  • Students who have checked in
  • Students on leave, shown separately
  • Students still expected

The board should be readable from across the room. Use large text, a few clear colours and no scrolling tables.

Protect student privacy

A lobby is public. Anyone walking in can read the screen, so show as little as possible. Use first name and initial only. Never show full names, phone numbers, fee details or marks. Tell students and parents what the screen displays.

Make marking attendance easy for tutors

A system only works if tutors keep using it. Marking should take moments on the phone already in their hand, with no register to carry to the office and no second entry later. Leave should be just as quick, so the board stays accurate.

Time the board around your batches

The attendance board does not need to run all day. It is most useful just before and just after a batch begins. BatchBoard, for example, shows each board from 15 minutes before the batch starts until 5 minutes after.

Use the quiet time for marketing

Between batches, the same screen can run your own content for the people waiting:

  • New courses and upcoming batch dates
  • Offers and admission deadlines
  • Student achievements, shared with permission
  • Centre notices and holiday timings

Keep each message short and keep your phone number on screen. Remove old offers on time.

What you need

  1. A TV in the lobby
  2. An Android TV or Google TV box connected to it
  3. Android phones for the tutors who mark attendance
  4. A stable internet connection

How to roll it out smoothly

  1. Start with one batch. Let one tutor use it for a few days and note what is confusing.
  2. Agree the rules. Decide who marks attendance, by when, and who records leave.
  3. Tell students and parents. Explain what appears on screen and why.
  4. Place the TV carefully. It should be easy to read from the waiting area and away from strong glare.
  5. Prepare your content. Have your course ads and notices ready before the first day.
  6. Review after the first week, then add the remaining batches.

Questions to ask before choosing a system

  • Does it work on the TV you already have, or with a low-cost box?
  • Can tutors mark attendance without training that takes days?
  • What exactly appears on the public screen?
  • Can you control your own ads and notices?
  • Is there a monthly fee, or a one-time cost?
  • Who helps if something stops working?
  • What happens if the internet connection drops?

Common questions

Does this replace our attendance records?

That depends on the system and your centre's requirements. If you must keep formal registers for a course or an authority, confirm what is accepted before you change anything.

What should the TV show at weekends?

If you have no regular batches, run your content loop. BatchBoard shows batch boards from Monday to Friday and your content at weekends.

Will parents be comfortable with this?

They are more likely to be if you keep the display minimal and explain it clearly. First name and initial are enough for a lobby, and anything more personal should stay off the screen.
