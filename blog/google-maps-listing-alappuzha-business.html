How to get your Alappuzha business on Google Maps

When someone nearby searches for what you sell, the map is often the first thing they see. Here is how to get your business onto it, and how to keep it there.

By Ad Pro Advertising · 18 November 2026 · 4 min read

The short version

  • Your listing is a free Google Business Profile.
  • Google ranks local results on relevance, distance and prominence. You cannot pay for a better local position.
  • Use your real business name. Adding keywords to it breaks Google's rules.
  • Ask real customers for reviews, but never offer anything in return.

Start with a Google Business Profile

Your place on Google Maps comes from a Google Business Profile. It is free, and it is the same listing that shows your phone number, hours, photos and reviews in Google Search.

Before you create one, search for your business on Google Maps. A listing may already exist, added by a customer or a previous owner. If it does, claim it instead of making a second one. Google allows only one profile per location.

Set it up in five steps

  1. Sign in at business.google.com with a Google account your business will keep. Avoid a staff member's personal account.
  2. Enter your business name exactly as it appears on your board and bills.
  3. Choose your category. Pick the most specific one that finishes the sentence "this business is a...".
  4. Add your address or service area. If customers come to you, show the address. If you travel to them, set a service area and hide the address.
  5. Verify the business. Google will show you the verification method available for your listing. Your profile is not fully trusted until this is done.

The rules that catch people out

Most listing problems come from trying to look bigger or rank faster. Google's guidelines are clear on these:

  • No extra words in the name. "Sree Bakery" is fine. "Sree Bakery Best Cakes Alappuzha" is not, unless that is truly your registered, signboard name.
  • No virtual offices or PO boxes. The address must be a real place you operate from.
  • One phone number you control. Do not use a number that forwards callers somewhere else.
  • Link to your own website. Google does not accept redirecting links or social media pages in the website field.
  • Few, accurate categories. Categories describe what your business is, not everything you sell.

What decides who shows first

Google says local results depend mainly on three things:

  • Relevance: how well your profile matches the search.
  • Distance: how far you are from the person searching, or from the place named in the search.
  • Prominence: how well known you are, including links from other websites and the number and rating of your reviews.

You cannot change where a customer is standing. You can make your profile complete and earn more genuine reviews. Google also states that there is no way to pay for a better local ranking, so be careful of anyone selling a guaranteed top spot.

Keep the listing alive

  • Update your hours before Onam, Vishu, Christmas and other holidays, using special hours.
  • Add real photos of your shop, team and work. Skip stock images.
  • Reply to every review, including the unhappy ones. Keep it short and polite.
  • Check that your name, address and phone match your website exactly.

Getting reviews the right way

Ask happy customers at the moment they are happiest: when the job is finished or the order is delivered. Send them your review link on WhatsApp so it takes one tap.

Do not offer discounts, gifts or payment for a review. Google removes incentivised reviews from Maps, and staff or family reviews can count as a conflict of interest.

Common questions

Can I list a business I run from home?

Yes, if you meet customers in person. If they do not visit your home, set a service area and hide the address. Your listing can then show the places you cover without publishing where you live.

Do I need a website to have a listing?

No, but a website gives Google and your customers much more to go on. It can explain each service, show your work and answer questions a short listing cannot.

What if someone else controls my listing?

Start by finding out who has access. It may be a former employee, a relative or an agency. Ask them to add you as an owner. If you cannot reach them, use the access request option Google offers on the listing.

What should I do about a bad review?

Reply calmly, stick to the facts and offer to sort the problem out. Report a review only if it breaks Google's rules, for example if it is fake or was not based on a real experience. Never offer a customer anything to change or remove a review.

We moved. What needs changing?

Update the address on your profile first, then on your website, social pages and directory listings. Change your signboard and printed material too, so everything tells customers the same thing.

A quick monthly routine

  1. Search for your business the way a customer would, and check every detail.
  2. Reply to any new reviews and questions.
  3. Add a few recent photos of real work.
  4. Check upcoming holidays and set special hours.
  5. Ask your recent happy customers for a review.
